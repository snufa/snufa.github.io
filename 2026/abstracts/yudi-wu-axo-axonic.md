# Axo-axonic inhibition suppresses Kenyon cell output in the Drosophila mushroom body

**Authors:** Yudi Wu, Albert Cardona
                           


**Presentation type:** Poster at [SNUFA 2026 online workshop (4-5 Nov 2026)](https://snufa.net/2026)

## Abstract

n the Drosophila mushroom body, the APL neuron provides widespread inhibition that helps maintain sparse Kenyon-cell (KC) activity, a key feature of mushroom-body function. KCs also form axo-axonic inhibitory connections with one another, but the functional role of these connections remains unclear. We used a two-compartment Izhikevich model constrained by the EM-derived PN-KC-APL connectome to test how KC-KC inhibition shapes KC axonal output.

We compared responses to an odour stimulus (A) with responses to the same odour combined with optogenetic activation of a second projection neuron population (A+opto), asking whether the recruitment of additional KCs further suppresses the output of odour-A-responsive KCs.

Enabling KC-KC inhibitory connections hyperpolarized KC axons and substantially reduced their synaptic output. However, recruiting the second PN population did not reduce output from the odour-A-responsive KCs below the level evoked by odour A alone. These results suggest that axo-axonic KC-KC inhibition acts primarily as a brake on KC axonal output through axonal hyperpolarization, rather than producing additional input-dependent suppression of an active KC ensemble.
