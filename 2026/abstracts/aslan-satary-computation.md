# No computation without representation: Abstraction/Representation Theory applied to neuromorphic and neural wetware computers

**Authors:** Aslan SAtary Dizaji
                           


**Presentation type:** Poster at [SNUFA 2026 online workshop (4-5 Nov 2026)](https://snufa.net/2026)

## Abstract

Neuromorphic engineering and neural wetware (synthetic biological intelligence, SBI) are the two
standing answers to the energy asymmetry between machine learning and brains. Both are routinely
described as “computing with neurons”, yet the sense in which each of them computes has never been
made precise. We apply Abstraction/Representation (AR) theory—the framework of Horsman, Stepney,
Wagner and Kendon for deciding when a physical system computes, and Horsman’s extension to
heterotic (multi-substrate) computers—to both paradigms. AR theory asks, for any putative computer:
is there a good, valid theory T of the device; a representation relation RT that can be run in both
directions (encoding and decoding); at least one fundamental operation; and a commuting diagram
between abstract and physical evolution that supports a compute cycle, in which the physical evolution
is used to predict the outcome of an abstract one? We show that digital neuromorphic hardware satisfies
these conditions by construction (its diagram commutes with ε= 0, its theory is a refinement stack
that reaches the transistors, and it scales by composition), that analogue and memristive neuromorphic
devices satisfy them only up to a calibrated ε with an increasingly phenomenological T, and that
present neural wetware systems—DishBrain/CL1, Brainoware, and the Neuroplatform—mostly do
not yet satisfy them: their device theory is phenomenological or normative rather than predictive, their
adaptive state admits no instantiation relation, and the cycles they actually execute are experiment
and engineering cycles rather than compute cycles. The one clear wetware compute cycle (organoid
reservoir computing) obtains it by moving the adaptive part of the computation into the representation
relation. The two paradigms are mirror images in the AR diagram: neuromorphic hardware is technology
(abstract-first, “history”), wetware is science (physical-first, “prophecy”); and at the level of composition,
neuromorphic-plus-host is hybrid while the closed-loop wetware architecture is genuinely heterotic. AR
theory thereby reframes the field’s open problems—credit assignment, benchmarks, energy accounting,
state persistence, and terminology—as precise questions about T, R, and ε, and yields an AR-compliant
benchmark protocol for SBI. The lesson for the SNUFA community is that the substrate does not decide
whether something computes; the representation does.
